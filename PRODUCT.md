# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Procurement Directors, CPOs, Category Managers, and Procurement Analysts at industrial companies. They buy commodity-linked products — specialty chemicals, paper & pulp, metals, food ingredients, freight — and negotiate directly with suppliers. Their context: a supplier has just sent a price-increase letter citing raw-material movements. They need a defensible, auditable counter before they walk into the room. They are analytically fluent, time-constrained, and deeply skeptical of tools that promise intelligence but deliver dashboards.

## Product Purpose

CostAdvisor builds buyer-side should-cost models from live commodity indices. Users decompose a product into its raw-material components, link each to a tracked market index, and the platform calculates what the product *should* cost today — given real index movements in energy, chemicals, metals, and FX. The result is compared against the supplier's actual price. The gap is the negotiation signal.

Success is a Category Manager walking into a negotiation with a number, a ranked list of cost drivers, and a one-page brief — all generated from the same live data their supplier is watching.

The platform does not replace the negotiation. It arms the buyer before they start.

## Brand Personality

Sharp · Trustworthy · Authoritative

Like a seasoned procurement consultant's analysis — not a startup pitch. The voice is precise, direct, and never impressed with itself. It knows things; it doesn't perform knowing things.

## Anti-references

- **Dark-mode neon / gaming aesthetic** — purple gradients, glowing neon accents, flashy visual effects that read as unserious for enterprise buyers.
- **AI-hype landing pages** — particle backgrounds, "powered by AI" hero badges, glassmorphism deployed as decoration rather than function. The brand earns credibility by showing real data structures and workflows, not by name-dropping AI.
- **Generic SaaS cream/beige** — warm-neutral palettes with rounded-everything that read as a generic B2B tool, not a domain-specific instrument.

## Design Principles

1. **Data is the voice.** Every claim on the page has a number behind it. Index data, commodity price movements, and real should-cost calculations carry more weight than marketing copy. Show the model; don't describe it.

2. **Expert confidence.** The interface addresses CPOs and Category Managers who spend more per year on commodity inputs than most companies have in revenue. Assume analytical fluency. Do not over-explain; do not simplify to condescension.

3. **Earned trust, not borrowed.** No AI buzzwords. No theatrical visual effects. Trust is built by showing real data structures, real workflows, and real methodology — not by declaring "enterprise-grade" in the hero.

4. **Analytical clarity.** Information density is a feature, not a problem. A procurement analyst's desk has Bloomberg terminals, index PDFs, and price history spreadsheets. The interface meets that mental model; it is a precision instrument, not a consumer app.

5. **Industrial gravity.** The users are responsible for significant procurement spend. The brand matches that gravity — not heavy or slow, but serious and competent. It does not feel like a Figma prototype.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Target**: WCAG 2.1 AA
- **Reduced motion**: Full `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` support — all animations killed, scroll-driven progress bar hidden, reveal transitions instant.
- **Keyboard navigation**: Focus-visible outline with accent color at 2px offset; skip link for main content.
- **Color**: Primary accent teal (#00A99D on light bg) must pass 3:1 for large text; body text (#333) on surface (#f5f7fa) passes 4.5:1.
- **Screen readers**: Section landmarks, alt text on all meaningful imagery.
