"Lock/hold decision off the forward should-cost"

The backward-looking half of this already shipped. GET /portfolio/buy-windows and /buy-windows/{cost_model_id} (backend/app/routers/portfolio.py:334, :349), _buy_signal at :308, frontend/src/components/BuyWindows.jsx, backend/tests/test_buy_windows.py. It compares current should-cost against the trailing-4-quarter average and flips cheap / neutral / expensive at ±3%. Its own docstring notes it does this without spot price data — which retires the premise in the old title. There is no spot data in the drop, and this slice does not need any.

What is left is the forward half. "Cheap versus the last year" answers where are we; a buyer signing a contract needs what happens next. With a should-cost forecast at combo grain, that becomes a lock-or-hold verdict over a stated horizon rather than a point-in-time adjective.

Grain. The verdict is per cost model, because a team buys a product — but the forward input is the combo-grain should-cost forecast, not a per-product projection.

One thing to check before extending _buy_signal. It takes series[-5:-1] off calculate_evolution as its trailing baseline, and that evolution window comes from _default_period_range -> _available_index_range (costing_engine.py:229, :173), which is max() over IndexValue with no kind filter. Once forward values exist in that table, the "trailing 4 quarters" is no longer trailing and the shipped signal degrades silently with no error. The forecast-storage subtask covers the fix; this subtask should not land before it.

Also worth stating plainly: the underlying values are base-100 index levels, not currency. A lock/hold verdict can quantify exposure in percent; it cannot quote a saving in money from this data.
