Two companies in the same category will genuinely build and price the same product differently — different suppliers, volumes, contracts, sometimes a slightly different recipe — so one rigid shared catalog is never going to fit everyone. The idea here is we ship a solid shared starting catalog, and any team can take a piece of it and make their own private copy to edit however they need, without it touching ours or anyone elses. This is the taxonomy that makes that possible — the extra subfamily layer the real catalog needs, plus that copy-and-own ('forking') model underneath it.

Where we are today: ChemicalFamily is flat + global (id + unique name — no parent, no team_id, no subfamily). Product links via chemical_family_id and already has team_id.

What we're adding:

A subfamily level → family → subfamily → product (the 22 → 91 → 257 grain above). Heads up: the family codes (F01–F28) skip numbers, so anything that walks them can't assume they're contiguous — that one has caught us before.

Forking: team_id (NULL = the shared platform copy, set = a team's own copy) on family + subfamily, plus an origin_id that points a fork back at the platform original it was copied from. That back-link is what lets a team rename their copy without breaking platform formula/index resolution.

Existing products should come through the migration still pointing at a family.

Done when:

A team can fork a platform family/subfamily, rename their copy, and platform resolution still works (because origin_id still resolves).

RLS keeps one team from reading another team's taxonomy.

Every existing product still maps to a family after the migration.

Sub-tasks:

Model ProductFamily / Subfamily (+ team_id, origin_id); repoint the Product link

Migration + backfill existing products

RLS policies (platform readable by all; team rows scoped)

Fork endpoint (copy a platform node → team node, setting origin_id)
