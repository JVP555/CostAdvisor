"Index projection service — per price series, emitted as a vintaged run"

The projection half of the Forecast epic. Method is not the contested part; where the output lives and what it is keyed to is.

Grain: price series. The series layer is region-baked, so a projection per series is a projection per (commodity, region) with no further qualification. Projecting at card grain double-counts the series that back several cards; projecting at type-code grain projects the same series repeatedly.

Output is a run, not a return value. Every projection carries the vintage (when it was made), the history window it was fitted on, the method, and its own uncertainty. That is what lets a forecast be scored later against what happened, and it is the only way "directional, honest about uncertainty" is a claim rather than a caption. It also means re-running is a new vintage, not an overwrite.

Two shapes in the source data that the fitting step has to answer for:

Roughly half the forward series shipped in the drop are the last actual repeated forward — a hold, not a view. Whatever you emit for those must be distinguishable from a fitted projection downstream, because rendering them identically tells a buyer the series is flat across the whole horizon.

A slice of series carry forward points with no history behind them at all. There is no tail to anchor to; decide what the service returns for those and make it explicit rather than empty.

Uncertainty comes from the data. ForecastArea.jsx's FORECAST_BAND = 0.015 is a placeholder for a page with no engine behind it; it should not reappear here in any form.

Depends on the forecast storage subtask for a place to write, and on W3-A for the series layer — do not fit against the region-stripped CommodityIndex grain.

Done

Projecting a series with history returns points with a vintage, method and interval; projecting the same series again produces a second vintage and leaves the first intact.

A series whose source forward path is a flat hold is marked as such in the output.

A series with no history returns an explicit no-history result, not an empty list.

Fitted output for a known series is pinned in a test, so a method change shows up as a diff rather than a surprise.
