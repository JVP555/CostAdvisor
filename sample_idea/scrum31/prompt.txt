"Quote & price-list extraction service — structured fields with confidence, confirmed before they land"

Turn a supplier quote or price list into structured, comparable fields, so the position engine has something to compare against instead of a buyer retyping figures.

The existing upload contract is the right one to extend. Every parser in backend/app/services/file_parser.py returns {"rows": [...], "errors": [{"row": N, "message": ...}]}, raises ValueError only for structural problems, and its router applies the result with dry_run plus update-only matching. parse_coverage_price_upload + POST /formulas/coverage/upload (routers/formulas.py:573) is the fullest example — it deliberately refuses to mint a row on a typo'd code.

Where a document differs from a spreadsheet, and why it matters here. A CSV has a header row and a stable row number, so an error can say "row 12, bad period" and a human knows what to fix. A quote PDF has neither: the same field turns up in a table, in a footer, or inside a sentence, and the layout changes per supplier. So the output needs, per extracted field, how confident the extraction is and where in the document it came from — and a human confirmation step before anything is written. Without that, a wrong number enters a negotiation carrying the same authority as a right one.

The fields that make a quote usable are the ones easiest to lose. Price alone is not comparable. Incoterm, named place, currency, unit of measure, volume tier and validity window are what let the position engine put the quote and the should-cost on the same basis; a numeric-only extraction drops exactly those and the resulting gap compares two different things. Quotes are also routinely multi-product and volume-tiered — one document is not one price.

Destination. Extraction output lands in the quote record, never in ActualPrice — see that subtask for why.

Open, and worth deciding with the quote record rather than inheriting from whichever library gets added first: the backend carries no PDF dependency today (backend/requirements.txt) and no blob storage at all — every upload is read into memory, parsed and discarded.

Acceptance criteria

An endpoint accepts a quote document and returns structured lines — product reference, price, currency, unit, volume tier, Incoterm, named place, quote date, validity — with nothing written until confirmed.

Every extracted field carries a confidence and a locator back into the source document.

Fields that could not be found are absent and listed, not defaulted — a quote with no stated Incoterm is reported as such rather than silently assumed.

A multi-product, multi-tier document yields multiple lines, not one.

A document that cannot be read at all fails structurally with a message, in the same shape as the existing parsers' ValueError path.

Round-trip test: an extracted quote is confirmed, lands in the quote record, the position engine computes against it — and ActualPrice is unchanged.
