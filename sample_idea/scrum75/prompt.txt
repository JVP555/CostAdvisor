"INT-1 · Intelligence derivation service"

Wave 3 · W3-E · Intelligence Engines. Depends on SCRUM-74 (Index Data Layer v2). Consumes three things it does not own: the combo trust grade from SCRUM-78, the volatility calibration from SCRUM-74 / DB-7, and the index_seasonal_factor table from SCRUM-69.

Estimate: was 8 pts against four outputs at the product grain. The grain moved and the output set changed — re-estimate at grooming.

What this is

One engine that derives every number an Intelligence ID card shows, from the weighted recipe plus index history. Going into a negotiation the buyer wants three things answered: what is driving my cost, how fast is it moving, and am I buying at a cheap or an expensive point in the cycle. Nothing today turns weights and index history into those answers at the grain the Intelligence library actually renders.

This is the service. The screen consumes it later and is not in scope here. Done means: the derivations exist behind an API, their JSON output is inspectable for any combo without a UI, and the test suite pins the awkward shapes rather than the happy path.

The grain moved: formula × region combo

The unit of work is the formula × region combo, not the product. In the schema that is a formula_region_coverage row — models/formula_template.py:156, unique on (template_id, region). A team product resolves to a combo (Product.formula_template_id + the cost model's region); it is not the thing being derived.

This is not a naming preference, it is what the two consumers need:

Intelligence library renders the platform formula catalogue, region as a selector. There is no CostModel behind those tiles. An endpoint keyed on product or cost_model_id cannot serve that page at all.

Portfolio works from a team's products, and gets there by resolving product → combo.

services/formula_resolver.py:175 evaluate_weighted_template() already computes at exactly this grain for one quarter, with coverage fallback and flattened chained components. It is the right foundation; what does not exist is the multi-period series over it, or anything else on this list. The multi-period path we do have (costing_engine.calculate_evolution) is CostModel-shaped, which is the grain we are moving off.

The outputs

Should-cost index series over a pinned historical window (base 100 at the combo's base period).

Weighted component breakdown — per-line weight, resolved index, level, contribution.

Percent change over the short and long windows.

Cycle position — percentile within the window, plus a verdict.

Seasonality — a 12-month profile for the combo.

Volatility percentile — the combo placed against the library.

1–3 are the existing story. 4 is specified below rather than left open. 5 and 6 are net-new and were discovered when the mockup was wired to the data.

Plus one the card shows that this engine does not derive: the combo trust grade is SCRUM-78's — it owns the derivation and the stored field. This engine reads that field into the payload and reports the inputs it was computed from.

Things that will bite

Margin sits inside the 100. Every combo that has lines sums to exactly 100.00, margin line included. evaluate_weighted_template's docstring already has this right — coverage.margin_pct is descriptive and applying it again double-counts. Carry that convention into the series code. Related, and worth knowing because it is the thing that would let a bad recipe through silently: backend/seed_combos.py:62 still validates the weight sum against a 99.5–110.5 band inherited from the previous convention. Tightening that is W3-A's, not ours, but if you are testing against seeded data assume nothing about the sum and read it.

Un-indexed and margin lines are part of the answer, not noise. For seasonality the blend runs over every line, weighted; lines with no seasonal factor (fixed costs, margin, anything unresolved) contribute flat 100 and are normalised by the combo's own weight total. That damping is the signal — a combo that is largely fixed cost genuinely has a flatter seasonal profile than its feedstock does. Dropping those lines instead of contributing 100 inflates the amplitude of exactly the combos that should look calmest.

Per-series seasonal factors are derived, not editorial. docs/CONTENT_MODEL.md specs index_seasonal_factor (commodity × region × month) as the derived table, and records that the drop's INDEX_SEASONALITY reproduces digit-for-digit from the index history — it is a cache of a computation, and the accompanying notes are three templates rendering it. That table is generated, never imported, and SCRUM-69 owns producing it. Blend from it here.

The volatility calibration is platform-level, and it is SCRUM-74 / DB-7's. The percentile is the combo's own month-over-month standard deviation mapped onto a library-wide breakpoint ladder shipped at newDocsFolder/seed/raw/VOLATILITY_PERCENTILE_BREAKPOINTS.json. DB-7 stores that ladder with a computed-at stamp and owns the recompute path — read the stored calibration and state which one you read; do not rebuild it here. What is ours is the mapping, and it has a trap: the mockup converts a ladder position to a percentile with a hardcoded ×5 step, which is only correct while the ladder has exactly its current number of rungs. Derive the step from the ladder length, or a re-calibrated table of a different size silently returns wrong percentiles.

The trust grade is read, not recomputed — and it is not coverage_tier. SCRUM-78 derives the combo trust grade from the resolution layer and stores it next to needs_review; it also settles which coverage_tier column that derivation reads (DROP_2026-07_ANALYSIS.md §7 item 3). The card's caveat and this payload must show the same field, so read it. If the payload also needs a coverage/proxy-density figure of its own, it gets its own name and a stated relationship to the grade — coverage_tier already holds a third vocabulary (free / good_proxy / weak_proxy / blocked) and data_confidence (CONF-HIGH/MED/LOW) is a fourth axis about pricing provenance. What this engine owes is the inputs: which lines are unresolved, which resolve through a proxy, and which source that came from.

"Is this line a proxy" has two sources that disagree. combo_lines.proxy_status and the proxy_status on the line's own type_codes row contradict each other for a material minority of indexed lines. Any coverage figure derived from one is reproducible; a figure derived from whichever column happened to be nearest is not. SCRUM-78 canonicalises one as part of the grade derivation — the derived payload echoes which one it read rather than picking its own.

The resolution layer has to be queryable, not incidental. Roughly a quarter of all indexed cost weight funnels into a single series through the type-code layer, and some type-codes resolve to no series at all. That means "this combo's number is really a bet on one commodity" and "this combo cannot be costed, here is the specific line" are both answers this engine should be able to produce, not facts that get flattened into a single index level. That resolution chain is SCRUM-74's to persist; consume it, don't re-derive it in memory.

Cycle position needs its window pinned in one place. Today the frontend does this client-side at IntelligenceDetailArea.jsx:94-98 — thresholds at 70/30, label hardcoded to "24-month", computed over whatever /costing/brief returned. The mockup has the same class of mismatch: buildCyclePosFor computes over the full history and the surrounding chart is labelled 24 months. Whichever window we choose, the verdict text and the window label have to come from the same constant, and there should be a test that they agree. The intended shape is three verdicts split at 70 and 40 (near the top / mid-range / near the bottom), plus a fourth case for a series that has not moved at all, which the percentile formula cannot express.

Not everything has an answer, and that is a valid response. A combo with no cost lines, no base-period anchor, or no priceable lines exists in the catalogue and will be requested. evaluate_weighted_template already returns a structured reason plus data_gaps rather than failing — keep that contract for the whole payload. The mockup throws on the combos with no lines; that behaviour is not worth porting.

Read path

The ID card is two calls, by design. GET /formulas/{code}/intelligence — SCRUM-76 / CON-7 — composes the editorial and dimensions read. This story ships the derived payload at combo grain. The card assembles from both; neither is a parallel copy of the other.

The shape of the derived half still has to be decided before it is built. One card's derived side touches series, components, cycle, seasonality, volatility, plus the trust grade it reads from SCRUM-78. docs/CONTENT_MODEL.md closes with the same point about the content side and asks for it to be decided before the API subtask rather than after. Same here: decide the shape (denormalised endpoint, materialised rows, or a documented N-query budget) and write the decision into the ticket before building it. Worth knowing what the library currently does — IntelligenceArea.jsx fires one /api/costing/evolution POST per visible card via an IntersectionObserver. That is tolerable against a team's cost models and does not scale to the platform catalogue.

Reference implementation

newDocsFolder/intelligence_mockup (19).html is a working implementation of these derivations against the real drop data. The relevant functions: computeFormulaSeries, buildDynamicsFor (3M and 24M windows), buildCyclePosFor, computeFormulaSeasonality, computeFormulaVolatility. It is a reference for the maths and the output shape, not for the window labelling or the missing-lines handling — both noted above. The same file also has computeTier (:1323), which scores unresolved lines by count with margin excluded — that is one candidate rule for SCRUM-78's grade, not a spec for this engine; hand it over rather than building it. Note there is an older file of the same name at the repo root; the one in newDocsFolder/ is the later copy.

Out of scope

Forecast values (SCRUM-70/21), the Intelligence UI itself, and the editorial content layer (W3-B). Owned elsewhere and consumed here: the trust grade's derivation and stored field (SCRUM-78), the volatility calibration table and its recompute (SCRUM-74 / DB-7), producing index_seasonal_factor (SCRUM-69), the type-code resolution chain and the concentration read (SCRUM-74), and the composed editorial + dimensions card read (SCRUM-76 / CON-7). Currency: the index series is a level, not a price — money only appears where the combo carries a base price anchor, exactly as evaluate_weighted_template handles it today.

Acceptance criteria

An API returns the full derived payload for a (formula, region) combo that has a coverage row, with no CostModel and no team product involved.

Given a product, the same payload is reachable by resolving product → combo, and the two paths return the same numbers.

A combo with an unresolved cost line names those lines in the payload; the trust grade in the payload is the value stored by SCRUM-78, asserted equal to the stored field by a test.

A combo with no cost lines, or with no base-period anchor, returns a payload with nulls and a stated reason — not a 500.

Seasonality is 12 values; a combo whose lines carry no seasonal factors returns flat 100s; a combo mixing seasonal and fixed lines returns an amplitude damped by the fixed share.

The volatility percentile is reproducible from the stored calibration, and the payload states which calibration it was computed against.

A test asserts the cycle-position verdict text and the window label are generated from the same window constant.

Output is inspectable as JSON for any combo, with no UI.
