"MON-1 · Trigger radar: contract/clause model + signal feeds + negotiation windows"

Est. 13 pts (was 8 — the two extra feeds, the window semantics and the alert wiring are new).

A category manager can't eyeball the book every day, so the moments worth acting on — a contract drifting toward auto-renewal past its notice date, a driver index moving hard, the gap between should-cost and the invoice creeping past what the team will stomach — pass unnoticed. This is the radar that watches for them.

Two things moved since this ticket was written, and both change the shape rather than the size.

The output is a time-bounded window, not a point event. "Brent moved 8%" is a fact; "you have until the 14th to give notice on the Q4 renewal, and the driver is running against you until the forecast turns" is a negotiation window. A window has an open, a close, a close basis, the drivers that justify it, and the products it covers. Point events can't carry any of that, and the whole reason a buyer looks at Monitor is timing.

There are more feeds than the three rules here. newDocsFolder/negotiation_mockup.html:107 states the Monitor scope as "index moves, supplier announcements and disruption signals across products in your portfolio — surfaced here to time negotiation windows." Clause proximity and gap are ours already; announcements and disruption signals are new sources, and neither has a live producer today (see Where the new feeds come from).

What this story delivers

A radar service, a store for what it produces, and an inspection API — exercised by tests against seeded data. It does not own a UI. frontend/src/pages/workspace/MonitorArea.jsx already says in its header comment that the trigger radar is deliberately Wave 3; leave that page where it is. Done means someone can seed a contract and an index move, run the radar, and read back exactly why a window opened and when it closes.

The pieces

Contract + clause. The only genuinely absent data in this story. Nothing in backend/app/models models a contract; CostModel.supplier_id is as close as it gets. A contract is a team↔supplier agreement with a term, and it usually covers several cost models — and one cost model is covered by successive contracts over time. So the grain is a contract row owning clause rows, with a join to the cost models it covers, rather than three parallel FKs on one table.

What the radar needs off it, at minimum: term start/end, auto-renew behaviour, notice period and the resulting notice deadline, price-review cadence, and a pointer to the indexation clause (which is the formula the team already keeps in formula_versions). The notice deadline is the only hard future date the radar has today — treat it as first-class rather than something recomputed ad hoc in the rule.

Index-move feed, at the resolution grain. This is where a straightforward implementation goes wrong quietly. Under the three-layer model (price series → index card → type-code), a move originates on a series; every type-code that resolves to it inherits the move, and it lands on every cost line naming one of those codes. A rule that walks each team's cost models and evaluates each referenced index independently — which is what _index_move_trigger in backend/app/services/alerts.py does today — fires once per product per index. One series backs roughly a quarter of the library's indexed cost weight, so a single move on it produces a near-identical event across a large slice of the book in one pass. AlertEvent.dedup_key dedups per subscription/target/quarter/direction, which does not collapse across products. Grouping by driver is the job the window is doing.

The resolution chain itself is SCRUM-74's (DB-5) — read the cost line → type-code → series path through its resolution endpoint rather than aggregating it here. What this story adds on top is the grouping and the window.

The attribution should also say whether the driver reached the line directly or through a proxy. Be aware that proxy_status is currently carried both on cost lines and on their type-code rows, and for a meaningful share of indexed lines the two disagree — read it from one place (the type-code side of the resolution layer) so the badge means something.

Blind spots must read as "cannot tell", not "no move". A set of type-codes resolve to no series at all, and the cost lines behind them carry real weight — it's a procurement problem, not a code problem (docs/DROP_2026-07_ANALYSIS.md §6). A comparison against a missing value returns falsey, so a naive threshold check reports those lines as calm forever. The radar's coverage state has to be tri-valued, and the inspection payload has to name the unresolved codes. A buyer who is told "no signal" on a product whose biggest cost line has never had a price is worse off than one who is told nothing.

Gap feed. Already computed — _gap_trigger in backend/app/services/alerts.py and the drift flags in backend/app/routers/portfolio.py. Consume it; don't restate the arithmetic. One thing to settle: an absolute-currency threshold only means anything where the team has a base price and an actual price. The platform index layer is value_kind = index_level (base 100), so nothing there is money. Store the threshold's unit with its value.

Buy-window feed. Shipped — _buy_signal / GET /portfolio/buy-windows in backend/app/routers/portfolio.py, and GET /portfolio/priority-matrix ranks what matters. Both are inputs to a window, not things to rebuild.

Where the new feeds come from

Supplier announcements and disruption signals have no producer in the repo and no live source in the drop. Real-time ingestion is explicitly out of Wave 3 — it sits badly against a monthly/quarterly index cadence (docs/WAVE3_PLAN.md).

There are two origins for this feed, and both matter: the dated current-events layer arriving via W3-B, and analyst manual entry. The W3-B material is editorial_block rows of type current_events_outlook, versioned, with as_of_date and expires_at (docs/CONTENT_MODEL.md). Worth knowing before you wire it: those records were largely authored to a single vantage date, so imported uncritically they all expire on the same day — the feed emits one burst and then goes silent.

So model the signal source as its own small entity with an origin discriminator (imported editorial · manually entered · future connector) and a manual entry path that works on day one. That keeps the radar unblocked while the sourcing question is answered, and it means an analyst who hears about a force-majeure can put it on the radar without a deploy. Open decision, worth raising rather than defaulting: whether supplier announcements are ever going to be an automated feed, or whether they are permanently analyst-entered.

Thresholds — one home, not two

The original ticket specified a per-team setting with a %-or-absolute unit and a 10% default. AlertSubscription.threshold_pct already exists (backend/app/models/alerts.py) — per subscription, percent only, default 5. Building the team setting as specified gives two live thresholds with different defaults and no rule for which wins, and users will hit that within a week of both existing.

Settle it as part of this story: a team-level default with a per-subscription override is the obvious reconciliation, but the point is that the radar and the alert layer read the effective threshold through one accessor. Whichever way it goes, migrate the existing column rather than leaving it stranded.

Alerts already exist — this is wiring

SCRUM-24 shipped: backend/app/models/alerts.py, backend/app/services/alerts.py, backend/app/routers/alerts.py (subscriptions, history, Slack webhook, on-demand POST /api/alerts/evaluate), plus evaluate_all_alerts in backend/app/tasks/alerts.py. So the hand-off is against real code, not a contract negotiated with a future consumer.

Two concrete facts about that code:

TRIGGER_TYPES in backend/app/schemas/alerts.py is a closed set — {index_move, gap, buy_window} — validated on subscription creation, and AlertSubscription's only scope columns are cost_model_id and commodity_id. A window scoped to a supplier or a contract has nowhere to point.

evaluate_all_alerts is not in beat_schedule. backend/celeryconfig.py carries scraper entries and no alerts entry; the task's own docstring asks for the wiring that was never done. Alerts have therefore only ever fired when someone POSTed the on-demand endpoint. Anything MON-1 hands to that layer inherits the same silence, so fixing it belongs here.

Delivery stays in alert_events — it is already the fired/delivered/dedup ledger. Windows are a different thing with a lifecycle, and they need their own store; don't build a second delivery log alongside the existing one.

Tenancy and permissions

Contracts are strict-tenant — the h8i9j0k1l2m3 policy shape. Market signals are platform-authored with team forks — the tx1a2b3c4d5e / rls1f2a3b4c5d shape. docs/CONTENT_MODEL.md has the table of policy shapes if you want the exact predicates.

Permissions need a decision: the alerts endpoints gate on costing.view, and the permission list in backend/alembic/versions/451dfc150b30_add_rbac_and_plans.py:20 has no contracts category. Contract prices and notice dates are more sensitive than a should-cost curve, and gating them on costing.view means everyone who can run a costing sees them.

Relationship to what's already there

cost_models.negotiation_state ({none, in_negotiation, agreed, under_review}, backend/app/schemas/collaboration.py:7) is the team's manual flag, with an audit-logged endpoint behind it. An opening window and that flag are talking about the same thing from two directions — decide whether a window suggests the flag, sets it, or stays independent, and write the decision down. Silently doing one of the three is the failure mode.

Dependencies, and what can start now

The clause/contract model, the gap feed, the manual signal path and the alert wiring depend on nothing outstanding — start there.

The index-move feed at the right grain needs the resolution layer SCRUM-74 owns (DB-5); this story consumes that read rather than building a second resolver. Forward-looking window closes need forecast storage — SCRUM-70/21 owns that table and the actual-vs-forecast discriminator; until it lands, a window closes on a clause date or it has a null close with the basis recorded as unknown. Don't synthesise a close date to fill the column.

Done when

A contract with auto-renew and a notice period returns its computed notice deadline, and a radar run opens a window that closes on that deadline with close_basis naming the clause.

A seeded move on a series that several type-codes resolve to produces one window per driver+scope with the affected cost models attached — a test asserts the window count, not just that something fired.

A cost model whose formula has a line on a type-code with no series reports coverage as unknown rather than as no-move, and the inspection payload names the unresolved code.

GET on a single window returns: the driver, the evidence values, the threshold applied and its unit, the cost line → type-code → series resolution path with proxy state, and open/close with close_basis.

The effective threshold comes from one accessor; changing the team default moves the fire boundary for subscriptions that have not overridden it, and a test asserts both halves.

A window that has already closed does not deliver.

Running celery beat shows the alert evaluation entry, and a test asserts the task name in beat_schedule resolves to a registered task.

Subscribing to a window-backed trigger through the existing alerts API works end to end and writes an alert_events row.

Not in this ticket

Monitor UI · real-time / streaming ingestion · a news connector · the forecast engine itself · the type-code → card → series resolution schema and its plain read (SCRUM-74 / DB-5) · forecast storage and the actual-vs-forecast discriminator (SCRUM-70/21).
