"Negotiation position engine — defensible target, attributed ask, and what we cannot claim"

The engine that turns a costed combo plus a supplier's number into a position a buyer can defend.

Three outputs, in order of how hard they are to argue with:

The target — the defensible price or price movement for this combo at this period, off the attribution service.

The ask — the distance between the supplier's number and the target, decomposed into the parts the attribution supports.

What we cannot claim — the portion of the difference the data does not explain, named explicitly rather than absorbed into the ask.

The third output is what makes the first two usable. A buyer who walks in with a number containing an unsupportable component loses the room on the first challenge.

Where the numbers come from. calculate_brief (backend/app/services/costing_engine.py:711) already produces gap, per-component drivers, volume-weighted impact and a narrative, and NegotiateDetailArea.jsx already renders and exports it via POST /api/costing/brief. That is the delivery surface. The narrative layer (services/narrative.py, rule-based with an LLM enhancement and a fallback) is also standing — talking points are a rendering of the position. The position itself is what does not exist.

Where the numbers cannot come from. There is no supplier cost data on the platform side: the market supplier payload in the drop contains no price, spend, should-cost or Incoterm. A "supplier's likely counter" derived from it would be invented, which is what NegotiateArea.jsx:15-18 records as having been removed once already. The supportable version of that idea is the inverse — given the attribution, which lines is a supplier most likely to point at, and what does our own data actually say about each. Anything stronger needs W3-D.

Composition, not authorship. Where an index dossier carries a negotiation pointer, or a negotiation_note editorial block exists for the formula (W3-A DB-7 / W3-B), surface it as evidence attached to the relevant line. Where it does not, the position is thinner and says so. The engine should not generate the missing content.

Comparability. A quoted price and a should-cost are comparable only on the same Incoterm, currency and unit. incoterm_normalizer.py, fx_converter.py and unit_converter.py exist; _normalize_to (costing_engine.py:337) shows the existing call shape. Skipping this compares two different things and the error is invisible in the output.

Margin. Read the epic's note before writing arithmetic. Combos carry margin as a line inside the recipe; the team cost-model path strips it out and re-applies it. Mixing the two conventions produces an ask wrong by the margin.

Acceptance criteria

An API call for combo + region + period + a supplier number returns: the target, the ask, the per-line attribution behind the ask, and an explicit unexplained remainder.

Attributed components plus the unexplained remainder equal the total difference — asserted in a test.

Every claim in the response carries what it rests on (line, resolved series, both period values), traceable without re-running the engine.

A proxy-backed line, or one that rode flat for want of data, is marked as such and is not presented at the same strength as a directly-indexed line.

Where quoted Incoterm / currency / unit differ from the combo's, the response states the basis it normalised to and the adjustment applied.

A combo with no base-price anchor still returns a movement-based position rather than an error.

A combo with no editorial evidence returns a position with no evidence attached — not placeholder text.

Output is inspectable via the API alone; no UI is required to verify any of the above.
