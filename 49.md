# What shipped since last Friday (Aug 28 → Sep 2)

A presentation script — not a code changelog. Each section says **what to click**, **what you'll see**, and **why it matters**, in the order you'd walk a room through it.

Two threads landed this week:
1. **The Index Data Layer** — a large data foundation project (12 build phases) that turns "raw commodity prices" into a trustworthy, explainable system.
2. **Brand polish** — the app's logo/nav now matches the marketing site, and a few SEO/session fixes.

---

## Part 1 — The Index Data Layer (the big one)

### The one-sentence pitch

*Every should-cost number in CostAdvisor depends on a raw commodity price feed. Until this week, roughly three-quarters of the catalog's cost recipes couldn't see real price movement at all — they just sat flat. This week's work brought in a much larger real data drop, built the machinery to trust it, and reconnected the whole engine to it.*

### Step 1 — Open **Indexes** (top nav)

Say: *"This is the single home for every commodity/FX feed we track."*

Point out the **status chip** on each row (Live / Proxy / Weak / Blocked / Stale). Click into any row to open the detail popup.

**What to say:** *"Before this week, a lot of these rows either had no data or were guessing. We now know exactly which ones are real feeds, which are stand-ins for something we can't buy yet, and which are outright unavailable — and we can say why for every single one."*

If a super-admin, point out the **"Derived indexes"** button — this is where a *composite* index (e.g. "Pencil = 60% Graphite + 30% Wood + fixed cost") gets built and calculated live from other indexes, rather than typed in by hand.

### Step 2 — Open any index's detail popup → the new "dossier" content

**What to say:** *"Click into a commodity now and you don't just get a price line — you get a dossier: what actually drives this price (with the lag and correlation strength named), which companies produce it and their market share where disclosed, how it splits by region, and — new this week — a seasonality chart."*

**Seasonality, specifically:** *"We don't import someone else's seasonality claims. We compute it ourselves from the real monthly history, so the number and the chart can never disagree. And where a claim in the source data said 'computed from years of history' but the series actually had none, we don't show a shape — we say so."*

**Volatility:** *"Same principle for volatility. We rebuilt the 0–100 volatility scale from the real spread of our own data instead of trusting an imported ladder — and we can prove it: 75 of 91 series' percentile scores moved once we used real numbers instead of the imported ones."*

### Step 3 — Go to **Formulas**

Open any catalog formula and look at its **recipe / coverage** table.

**What to say:** *"Every ingredient line in a recipe now carries a trust grade — High / Medium / Low / Blocked / Unrated — computed from real structural facts: does this line's price resolve to an actual feed, does it go through a stand-in proxy, do the weights even add up to 100%. Out of roughly 1,150 priced recipes, 587 got flagged for review — not the whole catalog, just the ones that actually need a human's attention."*

Click a flagged (amber/red) line to show the **"why"** — it names the specific commodities behind the flag and how much of the recipe's cost they represent.

### Step 4 — Go to **Curation** (account menu → "Go to" → Curation)

Three tabs: **Trust**, **Editorial**, **Dimensions**.

- **Trust tab:** *"This is the review queue from the previous slide — every flagged recipe in one sortable list, worst-first, so a reviewer works top-down instead of hunting through the catalog."*
- **Editorial tab:** *"We can now attach real written content to a commodity or formula — outlooks, supply/demand notes, compliance flags — with a proper approval workflow. Editing a signed-off note clears the sign-off automatically, so a stale approval can never look current."*
- **Dimensions tab:** *"This is new — a way to slice the whole catalog by non-price facts: what industry a product serves, what regulations apply, what substitution risk it carries. It flags exactly the raw labels we can't yet map cleanly, instead of guessing."*

### Step 5 — Go to **Dimensions** page (top-level, if you want the full explorer)

Click the **"Producers"** tab.

**What to say:** *"We now have a real company directory behind the scenes — separate from our own suppliers — so when the same manufacturer shows up under five spelling variants across our data, we recognize it's one company, not five."*

### Step 6 — Go to **Monitor** → **Radar** view

**What to say:** *"This is the negotiation trigger radar. Instead of just showing 'this index moved,' it opens a time-bounded negotiation window — with an open date, a close date, and the reasons behind it — and tells you honestly whether your coverage of that product's cost drivers is complete, partial, or unknown. If a contract's notice deadline is approaching, that becomes the window's close date automatically."*

### Step 7 — Go to **Intelligence**

**What to say:** *"The per-product intelligence view is now powered by the same trust and seasonality data we just walked through — should-cost history, a cycle-position read ('near the top of its 24-month range'), and a seasonality-adjusted view, all computed from real numbers, with an honest 'not enough data yet' state instead of a fabricated guess when the inputs aren't there."*

### Step 8 — If time allows: **Contracts** (account menu, permission-gated)

**What to say:** *"We added a real place to store contract terms and notice deadlines per product — this is what feeds the radar's negotiation-window close dates in step 6."*

### The honest caveat to state out loud

*"One data gap remains, and it's not a code problem — it's a data problem: 199 of 200 priced catalog combos still don't have a starting base price anchored to them. The tooling to import that is fully built; we're waiting on someone to actually supply the real prices."*

## Part 2 — Brand & polish

### Step 1 — Compare the app navbar logo to the marketing site (costadvisor.org)

**What to say:** *"The app's logo now matches the marketing site exactly — same size, same spacing, same shadow. It used to look like a slightly different, thinner version of the same mark."*

### Step 2 — Click the logo in the app

**What to say:** *"Clicking the logo now takes you to the marketing website instead of just refreshing the dashboard. And it's a two-way street: if you were already logged in and land back on the marketing site, it silently recognizes your session and shows a direct link back into the app instead of forcing you to sign in with Google again."*

### Step 3 — Point out the marketing site's color palette

**What to say:** *"We also fixed a color-consistency issue — two of our theme colors were basically identical shades of the same green, so the page read as one flat color. We pulled our actual parent-company brand color and gave the page real two-tone contrast, while keeping every color change passing accessibility contrast standards."*

### Step 4 — Mention, don't demo: SEO cleanup

**What to say:** *"Google had accidentally indexed our internal app pages and our API as if they were public marketing pages. We fixed that — those now explicitly tell search engines to stay out, so only the real marketing site shows up in search results."*
